# Mix Review Revision Priorities

Type: Mix Review Lab workflow
Tags: mix review, revision, feedback, priority, technical score, rms, peak, crest factor, stereo, tonal balance, action plan
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Treat a Mix Review result as a revision order, not a final judgment. Fix clipping and headroom first, then low-end balance, vocal or lead clarity, harshness, stereo translation, and only then loudness polish.

Try this:
1. If the review mentions clipping, true peak risk, or very low crest factor, turn down the mix bus and reduce limiting or clipping before changing tone.
2. If low end or low-mids are flagged, rebalance kick, bass, and muddy instruments in the full mix rather than boosting the master.
3. If the vocal, snare, or lead feels buried, automate level and clear masking before adding more compression.
4. If high end is flagged as harsh, check hats, sibilance, distorted layers, and bright reverbs before using a broad master EQ cut.
5. If stereo width is flagged, keep sub, kick, snare, and lead center-focused, then make supporting layers wide.
6. Export a new version with one or two fixes at a time and compare it against the previous review instead of changing everything at once.

Why it matters:
Testers often ask what to do after an analysis. A clear priority order turns KENN's measurements into practical mix decisions and avoids chasing loudness before the mix is balanced.
