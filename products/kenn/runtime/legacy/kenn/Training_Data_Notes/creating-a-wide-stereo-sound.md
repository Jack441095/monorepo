# Creating a Wide Stereo Sound

Type: Mixing technique
Tags: stereo width, reverb, EQ, compression
Status: Approved
Reviewed: 2026-07-01

Short answer:
Start by duplicating the basic sound and adjusting one version to be wide while keeping the other narrow. Then, add a second version with more reverb and slightly lower levels for stereo width.

Try this:
1. Duplicate the original lead sound.
2. Adjust the duplicated version to have wider stereo imaging.
3. Add reverb to the duplicated version.
4. Lower the level of the duplicated version in the back to create depth.
5. Use a multiband compressor on the second version with specific settings for mid and side channels.

Why it matters:
Creating wide stereo images is crucial for adding width and depth to your mix, making tracks stand out without overpowering other instruments or vocals. This technique helps in creating engaging mixes that are easy to listen to across different speakers setups.

Common mistakes:
- Not understanding the importance of reverb and its placement.
- Overcompressing the second version, which can lead to a harsh sound with no width.
