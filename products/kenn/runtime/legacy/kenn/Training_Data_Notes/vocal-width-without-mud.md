# Vocal Width Without Mud

Type: Mixing workflow
Tags: vocals, stereo width, width, wide, reverb, delay, doubler, microshift, high-pass, mono, mud, mixing
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Keep the lead vocal mostly solid in the centre, then create width on sends or supporting layers. Use a short stereo delay, doubler, microshift, or widened reverb return, but high-pass and low-pass the effect so low mids do not cloud the mix.

Try this:
1. Keep the main lead vocal centred and clear before adding width.
2. Add width on a send: short stereo delay, doubler, microshift, chorus, or a small room/plate return.
3. High-pass the width return so sub and low-mid energy stay out of the sides.
4. Low-pass or de-ess the width return if it makes S sounds or air harsh.
5. Widen doubles, harmonies, adlibs, and delay throws more than the main lead.
6. Check mono. If the vocal disappears or becomes phasey, reduce the width amount or change delay times.

Why it matters:
Wide vocals feel expensive when the centre vocal stays intelligible. Mud happens when stereo effects carry too much low-mid energy or when the lead vocal is widened so much that the middle becomes weak.
