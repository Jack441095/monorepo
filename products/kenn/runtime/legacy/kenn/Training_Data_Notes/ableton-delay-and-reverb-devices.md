# Ableton Delay and Reverb Devices

Type: Ableton device workflow
Tags: ableton, delay, reverb, filter delay, simple delay, feedback, space, diffusion, early reflections, decay
Status: Approved
Source: Ableton Live 12 Reference Manual (live12-manual-en.pdf)
Reviewed: 2026-07-06

Short answer:
Ableton's standard time-domain suite includes Delay (a simplified dual-channel delay with filter), Filter Delay (three independent frequency band delays), and Reverb (a classic algorithmic room/hall reverberator). These devices form the core space-design tools in Live for standard delay echoes and lush algorithmic tails.

Try this:
1. For Delay: place it on your channel. Link the Left/Right delay times (e.g., 3 sixteenth notes) or unlink them to create wide stereo offsets.
2. Toggle the Ping Pong button to bounce the delay feedback back and forth between Left and Right outputs.
3. Choose the delay transition mode (Fade, Pitch, or Jump) by right-clicking the device title bar: select Fade to prevent pitch warbling when changing delay times, or Pitch to emulate tape speed pitch bends.
4. For Filter Delay: place it on a pad or synth stem. Filter the inputs (Left, L+R Center, Right) to only delay high frequencies on the sides while keeping the mono low-mids dry and focused.
5. Set independent delay times and feedback levels for the Left, Center, and Right delays to generate wide, complex spatial reflections.
6. For Reverb: insert it on a Return track. Adjust the Input Filter to highpass signals below 150 Hz to prevent low-frequency mud from exciting the reverb tank.
7. Set the Decay Time (e.g., 1.5s to 3.0s) and adjust the Room Size to scale the algorithmic room characteristics.
8. Adjust the early reflections and diffusion network settings (using the Spin and Chorus controls) to add subtle pitch modulation to the reverb tail, softening metallic resonances.

Why it matters:
These classic devices are highly CPU-efficient and are the workhorses of standard tracking and mixing. Delay provides instant tempo-synced echoes, Filter Delay is perfect for frequency-separated spatial widths, and Reverb provides simple algorithmic spaces that fit well in standard mixes.

Common mistakes:
- Leaving the low frequencies unfiltered on the Reverb input, which causes low-end energy to wash out the mix.
- Changing delay times dynamically in Jump mode, which creates harsh digital pops and clicks (use Fade or Pitch instead).

When this does not apply:
- Complex space modeling requiring convolution rooms (use Hybrid Reverb instead).
