# Drum Layering Kick And Snare

Type: Drum production workflow
Tags: drums, kick, snare, layering, phase, transient, sample, punch, low end
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Layer drums by giving each sample a job. For kicks, separate sub weight, click, and body. For snares, separate transient, tone, and room. Check phase and polarity before EQ or compression, because bad layering can cancel the punch you are trying to add.

Try this:
1. Pick one main kick or snare as the anchor before adding layers.
2. Add only layers that solve a specific problem: weight, attack, body, width, or room.
3. Align starts and flip polarity if the low end or transient gets weaker.
4. High-pass top layers so they do not fight the anchor's low end.
5. Shorten tails if layers blur the groove or make the drum feel late.
6. Bounce or group the layers once the combined drum feels better than the anchor alone.

Why it matters:
Layering can make drums bigger, but random samples often smear transients and create phase cancellation. Clear roles and phase checks keep the drum punchy and easy to mix.
