# Mastering For Streaming Loudness

Type: Mastering workflow
Tags: mastering, loudness, lufs, limiter, streaming, spotify, delivery
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Aim for controlled loudness, not maximum smash: check peak and short-term level on the master, use a limiter with modest gain reduction, and verify the master still translates on earbuds after normalization.

Try this:
1. Finish the mix with at least 3–6 dB headroom on the master fader before mastering processing.
2. Insert EQ Eight on the master only for broad fixes (gentle high-pass on rumble, small mid dip if needed).
3. Add Glue Compressor or a light bus compressor (1–3 dB gain reduction on peaks).
4. Use Limiter with ceiling around -1.0 dBTP; adjust input until you hear pumping, then back off one step.
5. Compare against a reference track at matched perceived loudness (Utility Gain on reference), not matched peak meter.
6. Bounce 24-bit WAV at project rate; keep a less-limited “mix print” archive if the client may need revisions.
7. Note target loudness in the delivery email (many platforms normalize near -14 LUFS integrated — extreme loudness often loses punch after upload).

Why it matters:
Clients ask for “loud like Spotify” but hear crushing on their phone after normalization. This workflow matches what Audio_Too actually delivers.
