Short answer:
Resampling original sounds from software like Serum can save time by creating a library of similar tracks. Bouncing the resampled sound into a sampler allows for easy looping and modulation adjustments to fit specific track needs without needing to reprocess the entire sound each time.

Try this:
1. Print out the original sound in Serum with basic distortion, compression, and slight squashing.
2. Move the sound into its own sampler window and crossfade the end with itself to create an infinite loop.
3. Use FL Studio's filters within the sampler for additional modulation effects on pitch adjustments.
4. Start writing baselines by drawing quick pitch changes at the start and end of notes.

Why it matters:
By having a library of similar sounds, producers can quickly find and modify elements that work well in their tracks without needing to reprocess each sound every time they need to change something.

Common mistakes:
- Not regularly bouncing out processed sounds for easy revisiting.
- Overcomplicating the process by adding too many effects prematurely, leading to muddy or over-amplified results.
