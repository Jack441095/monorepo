# Creating a Clean and Distorted White Noise

Type: Mixing technique  
Tags: white noise, distortion, envelope, pitch, compression  
Status: Approved  
Reviewed: 2026-07-01

Short answer:
Start with filtered white noise, apply high and low cuts followed by two distortions. Adjust the filter settings for optimal sound quality.

Try this:
1. Begin with a short duration of filtered white noise.
2. Apply a high cut to remove unwanted frequencies above 5 kHz.
3. Follow with a low cut below 80 Hz.
4. Distort the signal twice, each time with a sharp envelope and pitch adjustment as needed.
5. Add a compressor for a spicy attack.

Why it matters:
This technique produces clean, distorted white noise that can be used in various contexts, including dubstep sub-bass sounds, providing a smooth and loud waveform without the harshness of square waves.

Common mistakes:
- Not adjusting filter settings correctly to achieve the desired sound.
- Overdistorting or undercompressing the signal, leading to poor quality or lack of attack.
