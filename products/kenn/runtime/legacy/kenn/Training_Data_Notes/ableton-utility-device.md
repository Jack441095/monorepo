# Ableton Utility Gain and Stereo Control

Type: Ableton device workflow
Tags: ableton, utility, live, gain staging, volume, balance, stereo, width, mono, bass mono, phase, polarity, phase inversion, mix routing
Status: Approved
Source: Ableton Live 12 Reference Manual (live12-manual-en.pdf)
Reviewed: 2026-07-06

Short answer:
Utility is a versatile signal-routing and gain-staging device. It provides precise gain control, stereo panning, channel swapping, phase/polarity inversion, stereo width manipulation, and a Bass Mono crossover function to center low-frequency energy.

Try this:
1. Load Utility at the very beginning of a channel's effect chain to perform initial gain staging, ensuring peak levels sit around -18 to -12 dBFS.
2. Use the Gain control to adjust track volume, leaving the main track faders free for automation.
3. Toggle the Phase Left (Phz L) or Phase Right (Phz R) buttons to flip the polarity of channels, checking for phase cancellation on dual-microphone sources (like drum overheads or acoustic guitars).
4. Turn the Width control to adjust stereo spread: 0% is full mono, 100% is normal stereo, and 400% is expanded side-only width.
5. Enable the Bass Mono switch on your master bus or bass group, setting the crossover frequency (e.g., 90–120 Hz) to force all sub energy into mono while keeping mid-range harmonics wide.
6. Use the Mono button to check the mono compatibility of wide tracks, listening for phase cancellation or disappearing frequencies.
7. Automate the Mute button for clean gating or track drops to avoid modifying the main track volume automation.

Why it matters:
Utility is essential for maintaining mix hygiene and mono compatibility. By centering the sub frequencies with Bass Mono and aligning phase polarity, it ensures your low end translates cleanly to club PAs and small speakers without phase cancellation.

Common mistakes:
- Automating track volume using the mixer faders instead of Utility's Gain control, which locks fader movements and prevents quick volume adjustments later in the mix.
- Forcing the entire bass channel to mono when only the sub-bass fundamentals need centering, which strips away useful stereo character in the upper harmonics.

When this does not apply:
- When harmonic coloring, saturation, or filtering is needed (use Saturator, Roar, or EQ Eight instead).
