# Mix Bus EQ Compression Order

Type: Mixing workflow
Tags: mix bus, master bus, eq, compression, glue compressor, signal chain, routing, mixing, before, after
Status: Approved

Short answer:
On the mix bus, use tiny corrective EQ before compression if you need to stop low-end build-up or harsh resonances from pushing the compressor around. Put broad tone EQ after compression if you are shaping the finished colour of the mix. A common chain is cleanup EQ -> light bus compression -> tone EQ or limiter for checking only.

Try this:
1. Fix big tonal problems on channels and groups first; the mix bus should only need small moves.
2. If the compressor pumps from sub or low-mid build-up, place a gentle EQ before it and remove the trigger problem.
3. Set bus compression lightly, usually 1-3 dB gain reduction on loud sections.
4. If the compressed mix needs a little brightness, weight, or air, add a broad EQ after the compressor.
5. Compare both orders at matched loudness. Keep the order that feels more stable and less smeared.
6. Avoid large mix-bus boosts into heavy compression; that usually means the balance needs fixing earlier.

Why it matters:
EQ before bus compression changes how the compressor reacts. EQ after bus compression changes the final tone. Separating those jobs makes mix-bus decisions easier and stops one processor from fighting the next.
