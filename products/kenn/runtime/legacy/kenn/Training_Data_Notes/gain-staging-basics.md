# Gain Staging Basics

Type: Production workflow
Tags: gain-staging, levels, headroom, mixing, clipping, meters
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Keep each stage in the chain at moderate levels so plugins behave predictably and you leave headroom on the master. Aim for healthy track peaks (roughly -18 to -12 dBFS on meters) before heavy processing, not “as loud as possible” on every channel.

Try this:
1. Turn down clip volumes or use Utility Gain so individual tracks peak around -12 dBFS before bus compression.
2. Use return tracks and group buses instead of stacking ten limiters on one channel.
3. Check the master occasionally in Ableton’s peak meter; leave several dB of headroom while mixing.
4. If Saturator or compression adds level, trim output gain on that device to match the level before the effect.
5. Export or freeze with the master fader at 0 dB unless you intentionally print hot for a specific platform.

Why it matters:
Clients often ask why mixes clip or sound “small” after limiting. A dedicated gain-staging note gives the chatbot a clear workflow answer instead of unrelated plugin manual pages.
