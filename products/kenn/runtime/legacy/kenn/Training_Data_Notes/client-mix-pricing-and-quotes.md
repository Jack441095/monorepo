# Client Mix Pricing And Quotes

Type: Client workflow
Tags: pricing, quote, quoting, rate, rates, discount, discounts, client, mixing, mastering, revision, stems, delivery, scope, business
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Do not invent a flat price from a vague question. Quote from scope: song count, stem count, editing/cleanup, vocal tuning, mix or master, deadline, revision rounds, deliverables, and whether new stems reset the job.

Try this:
1. Ask what is included: rough mix, stems, references, vocal tuning, editing, instrumental, clean version, mastered version, and deadline.
2. Split the quote into scope blocks: mix, master, editing/tuning, extra deliverables, rush turnaround, and extra revision rounds.
3. Include a written revision allowance, for example one or two scoped rounds, and state that new stems or arrangement changes are a new quote.
4. Take a deposit or written approval before starting client work; do not begin from a casual DM if the price is not agreed.
5. Put file requirements in the quote: labeled WAV stems, same start point, BPM/key notes, and reference tracks.
6. Send a short quote note with what is included, what is excluded, delivery format, deadline, and payment timing.

Check:
If the client has not provided stems, deadline, references, and revision expectations, Kenn should explain what to ask for before quoting instead of naming a random price.

Why it matters:
Pricing problems usually come from unclear scope, not the number itself. A quote that names deliverables, revision limits, and payment timing protects the session time and makes client expectations clearer.
