# Snare and Clap Layering

Type: Sound design
Tags: snare, clap, layering, phase, transient, drums, sound design
Status: Approved
Source: Audio_Too studio practice
Reviewed: 2026-07-01

Short answer:
Give each snare or clap layer one job: transient, body, noise, or width. Align the important attacks, remove redundant frequency content, and process the result as one sound.

Try this:
1. Choose a strong main sample before adding layers; layering cannot rescue a weak core indefinitely.
2. Use one short layer for attack, one tonal layer for body, and an optional clap or noise layer for width and tail.
3. Zoom in and align attacks by ear, then test polarity where low-frequency body overlaps.
4. High-pass wide clap layers so their stereo low mids do not weaken mono playback.
5. Route layers to a group for shared envelope shaping, saturation, or clipping, and keep the combined peak under control.

Why it matters:
Unassigned layers accumulate peaks and masking without making the hit feel larger. Functional layering creates a coherent drum that survives level changes and mono playback.
