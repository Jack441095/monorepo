# Drum Overhead Phase and Alignment

Type: Recording workflow
Tags: drums, overheads, phase, polarity, microphones, snare, kick
Status: Approved
Source: Audio_Too studio practice
Reviewed: 2026-07-01

Short answer:
Treat overheads as the main picture of the kit, place them deliberately relative to the snare, and check their polarity and timing against close microphones before processing heavily.

Try this:
1. Measure each overhead capsule to the snare centre when a centred snare image is the goal.
2. Record overheads alone and balance cymbals against the whole kit before adding close microphones.
3. Add kick and snare close mics one at a time, checking mono and polarity at matched loudness.
4. Move microphones or make small timing corrections only when the combined transient and low end clearly improve.
5. Do not automatically snap every microphone waveform together; arrival differences contribute to depth and cymbal tone.

Why it matters:
Polarity inversion can reveal a better relationship, but phase is continuous and placement sets it at the source. Visual alignment without listening can damage the kit's spatial character.
