# Transients and Crest Factor Control

Type: Mixing technique
Tags: transients, crest factor, punch, clipping, compression, drums, dynamics
Status: Approved
Source: Audio_Too studio practice
Reviewed: 2026-07-01

Short answer:
Crest factor is the gap between peak and average level. Preserve enough transient contrast for punch, then control isolated peaks with envelope shaping, compression, saturation, or clipping according to the sound you want.

Try this:
1. Compare peak and short-term level while listening to whether attacks feel sharp, soft, or disconnected.
2. Use clip gain or a transient shaper for a few unusually large hits before compressing the whole track.
3. Choose slower compressor attack for more initial punch or faster attack for a denser, softer transient.
4. Use gentle clipping on very short drum peaks when transparent peak reduction is more useful than envelope movement.
5. Level-match bypass and check the full mix; high crest factor is not automatically better or worse.

Why it matters:
Peak reduction can create headroom, but excessive control removes rhythmic definition. The right crest factor depends on the source, arrangement, and intended density.
