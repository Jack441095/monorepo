# Low-End Mono and Stereo Management

Type: Mixing technique
Tags: low end, mono, stereo, bass, sub, phase, correlation
Status: Approved
Source: Audio_Too studio practice
Reviewed: 2026-07-01

Short answer:
Keep the deepest fundamental region stable and centred, while allowing harmonics and higher bass layers to provide width. Do not force the entire bass mono if its upper stereo character is useful.

Try this:
1. Check the kick and sub in mono and inspect polarity or timing when their combined weight changes sharply.
2. Keep sub fundamentals and essential low kick energy near the centre.
3. High-pass or narrow stereo effects and side layers before they reach the deepest octave.
4. Use mid/side processing cautiously and level-match every comparison.
5. Confirm the result on headphones, one speaker, and small playback systems rather than trusting a correlation meter alone.

Why it matters:
Wide low-frequency differences can cancel in mono and behave unpredictably on club systems. Stereo harmonics can still create scale without destabilising the sub foundation.
