# Mix Review Low Headroom And Clipping Repair

Type: Mix Review repair workflow
Tags: mix review, headroom, clipping, premaster, limiter, mix bus, export, revision
Status: Approved

Short answer:
When Mix Review flags low headroom or clipping risk, lower the mix before making tonal changes. Find the loudest stage, remove clipping or excessive limiting, then export a cleaner revision with safer peak headroom.

Try this:
1. Turn down the mix bus, limiter input, or final output until the loudest section has safer peak headroom.
2. Bypass master processing from the end backwards: limiter, clipper, saturation, bus compressor, then broad EQ.
3. If clipping only appears on one group, lower or clip-gain that group instead of reducing the whole song.
4. Keep the premaster cleaner before chasing loudness; the next mastering step needs headroom and intact transients.
5. Export the loudest chorus or drop first, then re-run Mix Review before exporting the full revision.
6. Compare the new version against the previous bounce at matched loudness, not by which one is louder.

Leave alone:
- Do not add broad master EQ until the clipping source is fixed.
- Do not push the limiter harder to recover loudness before the mix is clean.

Why it matters:
Low headroom and clipping are usually gain-staging problems. Fixing the level path first protects punch, avoids false tonal decisions, and gives the next revision a clearer technical baseline.
