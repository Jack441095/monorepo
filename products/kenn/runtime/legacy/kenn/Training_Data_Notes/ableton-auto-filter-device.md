# Ableton Auto Filter Device

Type: Ableton device workflow
Tags: ableton, auto filter, filter, lfo, envelope, drive, analog modeled, cutoff, resonance, sound design, creative mixing
Status: Approved
Source: Ableton Live 12 Reference Manual (live12-manual-en.pdf)
Reviewed: 2026-07-06

Short answer:
Auto Filter is an envelope- and LFO-modulated filter device. It features lowpass, highpass, bandpass, and notch filter shapes, built-in analog-modeled drive circuits (OSR, MS2, SMP, PRD), and dynamic modulation routing to create movement, sweeps, or rhythmic filter effects.

Try this:
1. Place Auto Filter on a track (such as a bass synth, drum loop, or pad).
2. Choose your filter type (Lowpass, Highpass, Bandpass, Notch, or Morph) and slope (12 dB or 24 dB).
3. Select an analog-modeled circuit to add saturation: OSR (hard clipping), MS2 (screaming resonance), SMP (custom hybrid), or PRD (ladder filter saturation), and turn up the Drive parameter to add warm harmonic distortion.
4. Set up the Envelope follower: adjust the Envelope slider (positive or negative) and tune the Attack and Release controls to let the cutoff track the incoming volume level (perfect for dynamic auto-wah or transient-focused sweeps).
5. Set up LFO modulation: choose an LFO shape (Sine, Triangle, Saw, Square, or Random), sync the Rate to your song tempo, and increase the LFO Amount to sweep the cutoff frequency dynamically.
6. Create stereo width: adjust the LFO Phase offset (e.g., set to 180 degrees) to sweep the Left and Right channels in opposite directions, generating an immersive, wide stereo filter effect.
7. Create rhythmic steps: turn on the Quantize Beat control in the LFO panel to step-quantize the LFO sweep, turning smooth filter movement into stepped patterns.

Why it matters:
Auto Filter is a powerful creative sound-design tool. Its analog-modeled filters add warmth and grit to digital synths, while LFO phase offsets and step-quantization add organic width and rhythm to static pads and percussion loops.

Common mistakes:
- Over-driving the filter circuit (high Drive settings) with high resonance, which can cause sudden volume spikes and harsh feedback peaks.
- Setting the envelope release too slow on fast rhythmic tracks, causing the filter to stay open instead of returning to its baseline state.

When this does not apply:
- Precision corrective EQ cuts or boosts (use EQ Eight or a surgical EQ instead).
