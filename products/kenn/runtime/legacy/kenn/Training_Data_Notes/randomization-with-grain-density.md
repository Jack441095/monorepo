Short answer:
Start by setting grain density between 0.5 and 10 to achieve a balanced mix of randomness and control.

Try this:
1. Set grain size at 50%.
2. Duplicate the setup with identical settings.
3. Adjust grain length from shortest to about 250 milliseconds.
4. Change grain density between 0.5 and 10, starting around 10 for a more controlled mix.

Why it matters:
Randomization can add depth and energy to your tracks, but too much randomness might overwhelm the listener. Grain density helps balance this by providing consistent pitch variations without overwhelming the overall sound.

Common mistakes:
- Setting grain size too high or low.
- Not adjusting grain length appropriately.
- Forgetting to modulate grain density for added complexity.
