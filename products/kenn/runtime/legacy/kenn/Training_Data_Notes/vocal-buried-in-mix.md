# Vocal Buried In The Mix

Type: Mixing workflow
Tags: vocals, buried, masking, level, arrangement, eq, compression, mono, translation, mixing, sidechain
Status: Approved

Short answer:
A buried vocal is almost always level or masking, not a missing EQ trick. Before reaching for EQ, confirm the vocal is actually loud enough, then find which specific element is masking it and in which frequency range — the fix is usually to carve space in that one element, not to boost the vocal further.

Try this:
1. Check the raw fader level first — solo the vocal against the full mix and confirm it isn't simply too quiet; this sounds obvious but is the most common real cause.
2. Solo the vocal with one other element at a time (guitars, keys, other vocals, wide reverbs) to find which specific source is competing with it, rather than guessing from the full mix.
3. Once you find the masking source, carve a narrow dip in that element around the vocal's presence range (roughly 2-5 kHz) instead of boosting the vocal there — cutting the competitor is usually more transparent than boosting the vocal.
4. Check whether dense arrangement, not EQ, is the real issue — a bridge/hook with many instruments at full level will bury almost any vocal; try muting or ducking one or two elements during vocal phrases before adding processing.
5. Check compression relationship: if the vocal is compressed hard while the beat is not, the vocal's dynamic peaks (which usually carry it above the mix) get squashed away — try less vocal compression or more beat-side control instead of just raising the vocal fader.
6. Check in mono and on small speakers — masking that's inaudible on studio monitors can be much worse on a phone speaker, which narrows what's actually competing with the vocal.

Why it matters:
The instinct is to boost or brighten a buried vocal, but that usually raises the level of the masking problem along with the vocal. Finding and treating the specific competing source is more transparent and holds up better across playback systems.
