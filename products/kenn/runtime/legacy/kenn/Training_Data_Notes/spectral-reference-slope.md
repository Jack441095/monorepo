# Spectral Reference Slope

Type: Mix analysis workflow
Tags: spectral analysis, spectrum analyser, spectrum analyzer, reference track, pink noise, tonal balance, 3 dB per octave, 4.5 dB per octave, mix review
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Do not treat 3 dB per octave or 4.5 dB per octave as a universal target. A pink-noise-style 3 dB per octave slope is a useful baseline, but many finished references look closer to 4 to 5 dB per octave depending on genre, arrangement, brightness, and analyser settings. Level-match a few good reference tracks and compare broad low, mid, and high balance instead of chasing one fixed line.

Try this:
1. Pick two or three released reference tracks in the same style as the mix.
2. Level-match the references against your mix before reading the analyser.
3. Use a slow average or long-term spectrum view so short drum hits do not mislead you.
4. Compare broad bands: sub, low mids, presence, and air. Ignore tiny narrow differences unless you can hear them.
5. If your mix is much brighter than every reference, ease upper-mid or high-frequency energy before adding more limiting.
6. If your mix is much darker than every reference, check arrangement, vocal presence, cymbals, and saturation before boosting the master EQ.

Why it matters:
Spectrum analysis is best as a reference check, not a rule. The right slope depends on the music, so the analyser should confirm what you hear rather than replace listening.
