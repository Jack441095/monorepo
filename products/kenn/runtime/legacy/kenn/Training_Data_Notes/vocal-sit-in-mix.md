# Vocal Sitting In The Mix

Type: Vocal mixing workflow
Tags: vocals, lead vocal, sit in the mix, masking, automation, eq, compression, reverb, delay, mixing
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Make the vocal sit by balancing it against the instrumental first, then clearing masking and automating key words. Compression and reverb help, but the vocal usually feels right when level, midrange space, and depth all agree.

Try this:
1. Set a static vocal level with all instruments playing, not in solo.
2. Use clip gain or automation so quiet words stay understandable before heavy compression.
3. Cut masking in competing instruments around the vocal presence range instead of only boosting the vocal.
4. Compress in stages if needed: gentle leveling first, character compression second.
5. Use a short room or plate for glue, then a delay or longer reverb only where the arrangement has space.
6. Check quietly; if the vocal disappears, fix level or masking before mastering.

Why it matters:
A vocal that is simply louder can feel pasted on top. A vocal that has controlled dynamics, space in the midrange, and the right ambience feels connected to the record.
