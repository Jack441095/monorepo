# Harsh Hats And Cymbals Control

Type: Mix troubleshooting workflow
Tags: hats, hi-hats, cymbals, harshness, brightness, de-essing, dynamic eq, transients, drums, mixing
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Harsh hats usually need less level, fewer sharp transients, or dynamic control in the upper mids before they need a big dark EQ. Fix the balance first, then tame only the painful hits so the groove stays bright without stabbing the listener.

Try this:
1. Turn the hat or cymbal channel down 1-3 dB and level-match before adding processing.
2. Check the harsh band around 4-10 kHz with a narrow EQ sweep, then use a small cut only where it hurts.
3. If harshness appears only on certain hits, use dynamic EQ or a de-esser on the hat bus instead of cutting the whole top end.
4. Shorten hat samples or reduce transient attack if the click is louder than the body of the groove.
5. High-pass hat reverbs and delays so brightness does not become a constant wash.
6. Check on headphones quietly and loudly; cymbal harshness often hides at low volume and becomes obvious when the mix is pushed.

Why it matters:
Bright drums help a mix feel finished, but harsh hats make people turn the track down. Targeted dynamic control preserves energy better than making the entire mix darker.
