# Jack Gandy Ambient Reverb Routing

Type: Personal studio workflow
Tags: jack, reverb, send, return track, sidechain, compressor, space, mix routing, ableton
Status: Approved
Source: Jack Gandy Personal Workflow
Reviewed: 2026-07-07

Short answer:
Jack Gandy routes ambient reverb on a dedicated **Return Track** (Send) in Ableton Live. To keep the mix clean and prevent the reverb tail from burying the dry signal, he inserts a **Compressor** after the reverb and sidechains it to the dry source track (e.g. vocal or lead instrument).

Try this:
1. Create a Return Track in Ableton (Cmd+Option+R) and insert your reverb plugin (set to 100% Wet).
2. Insert a standard Compressor immediately after the Reverb device on the Return Track.
3. Enable the Compressor's Sidechain, and select the dry vocal or lead track as the input source.
4. Set the Compressor to react quickly (Attack 0–5 ms) and adjust the threshold so the reverb ducks by 2–4 dB *only when the lead plays or sings*.
5. Set the Compressor's Release to a time that lets the reverb swell up naturally in the gaps (e.g. 150–300 ms).

Why it matters:
Sidechaining a reverb return to the dry signal creates "breathing room" in the mix. The reverb is dynamically ducked while the vocal is active (keeping it clear and upfront), and then swells up beautifully to fill the empty space when the vocal stops.

Common mistakes:
- Inserting reverb directly onto the channel inserts and adjusting the dry/wet knob, which makes it impossible to apply sidechain compression or EQ to the reverb tail independently.

When this does not apply:
- When aiming for an organic, room-recorded acoustic sound where natural bleed and uncompressed reverb space are desired.
