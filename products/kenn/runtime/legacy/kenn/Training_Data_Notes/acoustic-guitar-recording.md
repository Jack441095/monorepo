# Acoustic Guitar Recording

Type: Recording workflow
Tags: acoustic guitar, microphone, recording, phase, room, stereo
Status: Approved
Source: Audio_Too studio practice
Reviewed: 2026-07-01

Short answer:
For a balanced starting sound, place one microphone 20–40 cm from where the neck meets the body and aim slightly toward the sound hole. Avoid pointing directly into the sound hole when boom is a problem.

Try this:
1. Tune the instrument, remove mechanical rattles, and choose a position in the room that sounds even.
2. Start near the 12th fret or neck-body joint and move in small increments while monitoring.
3. Move toward the bridge for more definition or farther away for a more natural blend when the room supports it.
4. If using two microphones, measure or adjust timing and check mono before committing.
5. Record a short performance test; pick noise and fret movement change substantially with playing intensity.

Why it matters:
The sound hole is often bass-heavy at close range. Placement balances strings, body, and room before EQ and avoids phase problems created by unnecessary microphones.
