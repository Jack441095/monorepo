# Drum Punch And Transient Control

Type: Mixing workflow
Tags: drums, punch, transients, attack, compression, saturation, parallel compression, clipping, mixing
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Drum punch comes from transient shape, envelope, balance, and controlled density. Preserve the attack, add sustain or weight with parallel processing, and avoid compressing so hard that the hit loses its front edge.

Try this:
1. Balance kick, snare, and hats before adding bus processing.
2. If the drums lack attack, slow the compressor attack or reduce compression.
3. If the drums lack sustain, use parallel compression and blend it underneath.
4. Use saturation or soft clipping for density, but level-match when comparing.
5. Shape individual samples before forcing the drum bus to solve everything.
6. Check that reverb, room, or cymbal wash is not masking the transient.

Why it matters:
Punch is mostly contrast: fast attack against controlled sustain. Over-processing can make drums louder but less impactful.
