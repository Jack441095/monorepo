# Randomization Techniques

Type: Mixing technique  
Tags: granular synthesis, random modulation, sample manipulation  

Status: Approved  
Reviewed: 2026-07-01

Short answer:
In the context of mixing and sound design, randomization techniques can be used to create a chaotic and dynamic effect. By applying random modulators to various parameters such as position, timing, pitch, level, panning, grain size, and density, one can achieve an unpredictable and engaging sound that adds depth and interest to the mix.

Try this:
1. Import a glitchy sample or preprocessed granular material.
2. Use a granular synth with multiple iterations of random modulation on parameters like position, timing, pitch, level, panning, grain size, and density.
3. Link the speed of the LFO to a macro control for added flexibility.
4. Experiment with different levels (e.g., 50%) to find the right balance between chaos and clarity.

Why it matters:
Randomization techniques can significantly enhance the realism and impact of sound design by adding unpredictability, making the sounds feel more organic and immersive in real-world environments where things don't always play out as expected. This approach is particularly useful for creating intense or dramatic effects that resonate with viewers without being overly realistic.

Common mistakes:
- Overusing randomization can lead to a cluttered mix that lacks focus.
- Not properly linking parameters (like jitter and smooth) can result in inconsistent results, making the sound feel unnatural.
