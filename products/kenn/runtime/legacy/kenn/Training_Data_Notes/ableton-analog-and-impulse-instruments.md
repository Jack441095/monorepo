# Ableton Analog and Impulse Instruments

Type: Ableton device workflow
Tags: ableton, analog, impulse, synthesizer, drum sampler, subtractive, filters, envelopes, routing, sound design
Status: Approved
Source: Ableton Live 12 Reference Manual (live12-manual-en.pdf)
Reviewed: 2026-07-06

Short answer:
Analog and Impulse are two of Ableton's classic instruments. Analog is a virtual subtractive synthesizer that emulates the architecture and sound of vintage analog hardware. Impulse is a drum sampler that features eight drum pads with independent filter, envelope, pitch, and time-stretching controls.

Try this:
1. For Analog: place it on a MIDI track. Select your waveforms on Osc 1 and Osc 2 (Sine, Triangle, Saw, or Noise).
2. Configure the dual multi-mode filters (Fil 1 and Fil 2): adjust the routing slider to run the filters in Series (Osc -> Fil 1 -> Fil 2) or Parallel (Osc -> both filters independently) to sculpt complex frequency responses.
3. Map the Filter Envelope to the cutoff frequency (using the Env knob in the filter section) to create classic pluck or sweep effects.
4. For Impulse: place it on a MIDI track and drag drum samples onto the eight pads.
5. Select a pad and use the Stretch control to compress or expand the sample length in real time to match the song's tempo without shifting the pitch.
6. Enable the Saturation or Filter on individual pads to add drive and control high-end bleed.
7. Route individual pads to separate audio tracks in your Ableton session to apply independent mixing chains to your kick, snare, and percussion.

Why it matters:
Analog provides classic virtual analog subtractive synthesis with low CPU consumption, making it ideal for standard synth leads, basses, and pads. Impulse is a fast, performance-friendly alternative to Drum Rack, offering direct time-stretch and frequency modulation controls per sample slot that are perfect for creative beat design.

Common mistakes:
- Overlapping sub-bass frequencies on Analog by running both oscillators at low octaves without filtering.
- Routing all drum pads through the main output of Impulse instead of using multi-out tracks, making it impossible to EQ or compress the kick and snare independently.

When this does not apply:
- Complex wavetable synthesis or advanced multi-sample instrument modeling (use Wavetable or Sampler instead).
