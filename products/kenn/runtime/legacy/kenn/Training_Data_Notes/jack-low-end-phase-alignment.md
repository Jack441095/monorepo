# Jack Gandy Low-End Phase Alignment

Type: Personal studio workflow

Reviewed: 2026-07-07

Short answer:
Jack Gandy aligns the phase of the kick drum and sub-bass fundamental to ensure maximum low-end power and translation. He achieves this by manually zooming in on the waveforms in Ableton's arrangement view to align the zero-crossings, and toggling polarity (Utility phase invert) to check for constructive summation.

Try this:
1. Drag the kick and bass tracks next to each other in Ableton's Arrangement view.
2. Zoom in closely to a section where the kick hit aligns with a bass note.
3. Look at the starting wave cycle of the kick and the sub bass:
   - If both wave cycles start moving in the positive direction (upwards), they are in phase.
   - If one starts positive (up) and the other negative (down), they are out of phase, causing cancellation.
4. If they are out of phase, insert a Utility device on the bass track and toggle the Phase Left (Phz L) and Phase Right (Phz R) buttons to invert the polarity.
5. Listen in mono: choose the phase setting that produces the loudest, most solid low-end thump.

Why it matters:
Because kick drums and sub-bass fundamentally share the 30–90 Hz frequency range, any phase misalignment causes destructive interference (cancellation). Proper alignment ensures the low end sums constructively, translating cleanly to club sound systems and subwoofer setups.

Common mistakes:
- Relying entirely on visual wave alignment without listening to the summing result in mono, which can lead to mistakes if the phase changes across different notes.
- Automating track delays without checking compatibility.

When this does not apply:
- When the kick and bass do not play at the same time (e.g. call-and-response patterns or offbeat basslines).
