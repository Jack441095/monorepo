Short answer:
Jack Gandy runs two distinct vocal recording chains depending on the style and vocalist:
1. **Dynamic & Aggressive Vocals**: **Shure SM7B** dynamic microphone connected to a preamp with healthy gain (e.g. Cloudlifter or Apollo Unison preamp).
2. **Detailed & Modern Vocals**: **Neumann TLM 103** large-diaphragm condenser microphone.
Both microphones route directly into an **Universal Audio Apollo Twin** interface using clean input settings or Unison preamps.

Try this:
1. For SM7B: Place the mic 2–6 inches from the vocalist. Ensure the preamp has at least 60 dB of clean gain (or use a Cloudlifter/inline booster) to keep the noise floor low.
2. For TLM 103: Place the mic 6–10 inches from the vocalist, and use a high-quality pop filter to prevent plosives from overloading the sensitive capsule.
3. Track into the Apollo Twin, using Unison preamps (like the UA 610-B or Neve 1073) to add subtle harmonic warmth on input.

Why it matters:
The SM7B excels at dynamic, aggressive, or loud vocals (rap, rock) because its dynamic capsule handles high SPL and rejects room noise. The TLM 103 has a bright, highly detailed top-end that captures breath and detail in intimate or modern pop vocals.

Common mistakes:
- Using the TLM 103 in untreated or noisy rooms, which captures excessive room reflection and background noise due to its high sensitivity.
- Running the SM7B into a low-gain preamp, resulting in a weak signal and a high noise floor.

When this does not apply:
- When recording instruments or guitars (where other specialized microphones like the SM57 or Coles ribbon mics are preferred).
