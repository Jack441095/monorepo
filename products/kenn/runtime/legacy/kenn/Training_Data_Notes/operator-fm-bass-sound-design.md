# Operator FM Bass Sound Design

Type: Sound design workflow
Tags: operator, fm, bass, sub, sound design, ableton, synth, harmonics, saturation
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Use Operator for FM bass by starting with one clean low carrier, then adding a quieter modulator for harmonics. Keep the sub stable, shape the midrange with FM amount and filtering, then add saturation in parallel or after the synth so the bass reads on smaller speakers.

Try this:
1. Load Operator and choose an algorithm where oscillator B modulates oscillator A.
2. Set oscillator A to a sine or triangle in the bass octave.
3. Set oscillator B one or two octaves above A and raise its level slowly until the bass gains bite.
4. Keep the very low sub simple; use EQ or a rack split if the FM movement makes the sub unstable.
5. Add Saturator, Pedal, or soft clipping after Operator for audible harmonics.
6. Check the patch in mono and against the kick before committing it.

Why it matters:
FM bass can become exciting quickly, but too much modulation makes the low end uneven. Separating a stable sub from controlled midrange harmonics keeps the sound powerful without making the mix cloudy.
