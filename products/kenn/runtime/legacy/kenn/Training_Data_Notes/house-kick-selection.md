# Choosing A Kick For House And Deep House

Type: Sound selection workflow
Tags: house, house music, deep house, kick, kick drum, sample selection, dance music, low end, club, groove, arrangement
Status: Approved

Short answer:
For house and deep house, choose one kick that supplies the groove before trying to make it huge in solo. Start with a short, consistent kick whose low-end pitch and tail leave room for the bass line. The right choice is the one that reads at low volume, repeats cleanly at the song tempo, and still works when the bass enters—not necessarily the deepest or loudest sample.

Try this:
1. Put two or three candidate kicks on the actual busiest section of the track, at equal peak or perceived level. Do not audition a large folder in solo.
2. For each candidate, listen to the relationship with the bass: note whether the kick's tail overlaps the next bass note or whether its fundamental makes the low end smaller when both play.
3. Choose the role first: a short, punchy kick with a separate bass/sub is usually easier to control; a longer, rounder kick needs more intentional space in the bass pattern.
4. Check the kick quietly and on a small speaker. If the pulse disappears, choose a sample with a clearer body or attack rather than only boosting sub.
5. Collapse to mono and compare the candidates again. Reject a kick whose low end changes dramatically with the bass or that only sounds good because it is louder.
6. Commit to the strongest single kick. Layer only if you can name the missing role—attack, body, or weight—and verify that the layer improves the whole groove at matched loudness.

Listening checks:
- In the full groove, every kick should be audible without the bass ducking so far that the rhythm loses continuity.
- The tail should support the tempo rather than blur the next kick or bass note.
- At matched loudness, the preferred sample should make the track move more clearly, not merely sound bigger in solo.

Avoid:
- Choosing by waveform size, sample-pack label, or solo loudness.
- Starting with a long 808-style tail when the arrangement already has a sustained sub bass.
- Layering several kicks before checking timing, polarity, mono, and the kick/bass relationship.
- Copying a reference kick's exact pitch or tail without considering the song's bassline, key, tempo, and arrangement.

Why it matters:
House kick selection is an arrangement and low-end relationship decision. A sample that feels impressive alone can fight the bass, blur the groove, or fail on smaller playback. Auditioning only a few candidates in context keeps the choice musical, reversible, and easy to mix.
