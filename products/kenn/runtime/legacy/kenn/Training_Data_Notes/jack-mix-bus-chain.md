# Jack Gandy Standard Mix-Bus Chain

Type: Personal studio workflow

Status: Approved

Reviewed: 2026-07-07

Short answer:
Jack Gandy's standard master mix-bus processing chain in Ableton Live consists of three stock devices in order: **Glue Compressor** (for subtle dynamic cohesion), **EQ Eight** (for master tonal balancing), and **Saturator** (for soft-clipping transients before the final stage).

Try this:
1. Insert the Glue Compressor: Set attack to 30ms (to let the kick transient breathe), release to Auto, and ratio to 4:1. Adjust threshold to get at most 1–2 dB of gain reduction on the loudest parts of the song.
2. Insert EQ Eight: Use a high-pass filter at 20 Hz (48 dB/oct) to clean up sub-low rumble. Add a wide, subtle high shelf boost (e.g., 0.5–1 dB at 10 kHz) if the mix needs a bit of air.
3. Insert Saturator: Enable the soft-clipping toggle, set drive to 0 dB, and use it to catch and round off fast transients before they hit the mastering stage.
4. Keep these plugins active *from the start of the mix* so you mix "into" the master chain.

Why it matters:
Mixing "into" a master compressor and soft-clipper helps you make better balancing decisions early in the process. It glues the stereo elements together naturally and rounds off transients, meaning you don't have to work the mastering limiter as hard later.

Common mistakes:
- Stacking heavy brickwall limiters or dynamic processors on the master bus while mixing, which crushes the dynamics and masks mix balance issues.

When this does not apply:
- When preparing clean stems for an external mastering engineer (bypass the master bus chain or consult the engineer first).
