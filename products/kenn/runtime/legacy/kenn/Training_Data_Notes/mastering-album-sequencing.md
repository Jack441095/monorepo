# Mastering Album Sequencing and Consistency

Type: Mastering workflow
Tags: mastering, album, ep, sequencing, perceived loudness, integrated loudness, track transitions, spacing, metadata, consistency
Status: Approved
Source: Audio_Too studio practice
Reviewed: 2026-07-01

Short answer:
Master a group of songs as a sequence, comparing perceived loudness, tonal balance, stereo image, and spacing in context. Equal meter readings do not guarantee that tracks feel equally loud.

Try this:
1. Import final mixes into one session at their native resolution and establish the intended running order.
2. Level-match transitions by ear at a consistent monitor level, using integrated loudness only as supporting evidence.
3. Compare low-end weight, vocal presence, brightness, width, and peak behaviour across adjacent tracks.
4. Set starts, ends, fades, and gaps while listening through complete transitions.
5. Export versioned masters and verify titles, order, sample rate, bit depth, and requested metadata against the delivery sheet.

Why it matters:
Listeners experience the release as a sequence. Track-by-track maximisation can create distracting jumps and undermine the musical arc even when each file sounds strong alone.
