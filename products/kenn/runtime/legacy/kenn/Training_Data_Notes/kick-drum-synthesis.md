# Kick Drum Synthesis

Type: Sound design
Tags: kick, drum synthesis, pitch envelope, transient, sub, operator, sampler
Status: Approved
Source: Audio_Too studio practice
Reviewed: 2026-07-01

Short answer:
Build a synthetic kick from a fast downward pitch sweep for the knock, a controlled low-frequency tail for weight, and an optional short noise or click layer for definition.

Try this:
1. Start with a sine wave and apply a steep pitch envelope that falls from a higher frequency to the intended fundamental in roughly 20–80 ms.
2. Shape the amplitude envelope so the tail suits the tempo and does not overlap the next kick or bass phrase.
3. Add a very short filtered noise or click layer only if the kick needs definition on small speakers.
4. Tune the settled tail deliberately, but judge it against the bass line rather than forcing every transient to one exact note.
5. Saturate or clip gently for density, then check polarity and phase if layers are combined.

Why it matters:
The pitch envelope defines the perceived punch while the settled tail defines low-end weight. Treating those parts separately makes the kick easier to fit into an arrangement.
