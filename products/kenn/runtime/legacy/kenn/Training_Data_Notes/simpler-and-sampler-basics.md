# Simpler and Sampler Basics

Type: Sound design workflow
Tags: simpler, sampler, ableton, slice, one-shot, loop, chop, warp, pitch, mapping, sample
Status: Approved
Source: Ableton Live 12 Manual
Reviewed: 2026-07-01

Short answer:
Simpler is a single-sample player with a trimmer, loop controls, and basic synthesis (filter, LFO, envelopes). Sampler is the multi-sample version — it maps samples across pitch and velocity ranges and handles keygroups. Use Simpler for chopping, one-shots, and quick sound design. Use Sampler when you need a fully chromatic instrument mapped across the keyboard.

Try this:
1. Drag any audio clip from the arrangement into a Simpler instrument track to load it instantly.
2. Set the mode: Classic (pitched playback), 1-Shot (plays to end regardless of note length), or Slice (splits on transients for drum chop or vocal re-pitching).
3. In Classic mode, set the loop region in the waveform display and enable Loop to sustain a pad. Snap points ensure clean loop boundaries.
4. Use the Start and End markers to trim to only the useful part of a sample.
5. For drum chops, use Slice mode: Ableton auto-detects transients and maps each slice to a MIDI note. You can then trigger individual slices from a MIDI clip or drum rack.
6. To go multi-sample: right-click the Simpler title bar → Convert to Sampler. This unlocks keygroup zones, velocity layers, and round-robin.
7. In Sampler, drag samples into zone view and set their pitch and velocity ranges to build a realistic instrument.

Why it matters:
Simpler is the fastest way to make a sampled instrument in Ableton. Slice mode turns any loop — a drum break, a chord stab, a vocal — into a playable MIDI instrument without any third-party tools.

Common mistakes:
- Leaving interpolation on Low quality for pitched material — use High or Repitch for better pitch accuracy.
- Not checking mono compatibility after widening a sampled pad with Simpler's Stereo Spread.
- Using Sampler when Simpler would do — Simpler has lower CPU cost.

When this does not apply:
For synthesis, use Wavetable, Operator, or Analog. Simpler and Sampler are for sample-based sound design.
