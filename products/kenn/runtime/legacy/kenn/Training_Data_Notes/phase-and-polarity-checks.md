# Phase And Polarity Checks

Type: Recording and mixing workflow
Tags: phase, polarity, mono, drums, vocals, bass, disappear, cancellation, recording, mixing
Status: Approved

Short answer:
If bass, drums, or layered sounds lose weight or disappear in mono, check phase, polarity, and cancellation before reaching for EQ. Flip polarity, adjust timing, or remove layers that fight the main sound.

Try this:
1. Collapse the mix or layered sound to mono and listen for lost low-end, hollow tone, or weak punch.
2. Flip polarity on one layer or mic channel and keep the setting that sounds fuller.
3. Nudge layers only if they are clearly meant to hit together, such as kick samples or multi-mic drums.
4. Avoid aligning everything visually if it makes the groove or tone worse.
5. For bass layers, keep the sub layer mono and make sure mid layers do not cancel the fundamental.
6. Re-check after stereo widening plugins, chorus, Haas delays, or modulation effects.

Why it matters:
Phase problems can make a mix feel thin even when the individual tracks sound good soloed. Mono checks reveal issues that stereo listening can hide.
