Short answer:
When placing sounds, consider both their positions in stereo field and frequency spectrum. Focus on resonant areas to create separation and balance.

Try this:
1. Use the sidechain function of your audio interface or plugin to separate elements based on their frequencies.
2. Adjust the volume levels of each element to ensure they complement rather than clash with one another.
3. Experiment with different placements in both stereo field and frequency spectrum to achieve a balanced mix.
4. Pay attention to the fundamental frequency of sounds, as it becomes less important towards higher frequencies.

Why it matters:
By understanding how your sound's frequency signature interacts with other elements, you can create a more cohesive and enjoyable listening experience for your audience.

Common mistakes:
- Overemphasizing high-end frequencies in bass or kick drums without considering their impact on the overall mix.
- Ignoring the fundamental frequency of sounds, which becomes less important as they move into higher ranges.
