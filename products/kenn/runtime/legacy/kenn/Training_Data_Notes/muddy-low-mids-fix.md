# Fix Muddy Low-Mids In A Mix

Type: Mixing workflow
Tags: mixing, eq, low-mids, mud, clarity, balance, workflow
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Mud is usually stacked energy around 200–500 Hz on several elements at once. Cut narrow on supporting parts, not on everything, and check the mix in mono after each change.

Try this:
1. Solo the full mix and identify if mud is constant or only in dense sections (chorus).
2. High-pass guitars, keys, and pads that do not need body (start around 100–150 Hz, adjust by ear).
3. On bass and kick, cut broad only if needed — often reduce 250–400 Hz on **guitars/keys** first, not on bass.
4. Use EQ Eight bell mode: sweep boosted 300 Hz until it sounds worse, then cut 2–4 dB at that frequency on the offender.
5. Check bus compression is not adding constant gain reduction that thickens the low-mids.
6. Toggle mono (Utility on master or headphones) — if mud gets worse in mono, reduce competing parts rather than adding top end.
7. Re-level after EQ; cuts change perceived loudness.

Why it matters:
“Sounds muddy” is one of the most common client mix notes. A dedicated workflow beats random manual EQ pages in retrieval.
