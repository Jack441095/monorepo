# Vocal Microphone Placement and Proximity Effect

Type: Recording workflow
Tags: vocal, microphone, placement, proximity effect, pop filter, room, recording
Status: Approved
Source: Audio_Too studio practice
Reviewed: 2026-07-01

Short answer:
Start with the singer 15–20 cm from a cardioid microphone with a pop filter between them. Adjust distance and angle before reaching for EQ: closer sounds fuller but increases proximity effect, plosives, and mouth noise.

Try this:
1. Put the microphone away from walls and reflective corners, with its least-sensitive rear side facing the noisiest or most reflective part of the room.
2. Place a pop filter 5–10 cm from the capsule and keep the singer another 10–15 cm behind it.
3. Aim slightly above or to the side of the mouth if plosives and sibilance are excessive.
4. Move the singer back in small steps when the low mids sound boomy; move closer only when the room is controlled and more intimacy is useful.
5. Mark the standing position and record a short test at performance volume before the full take.

Why it matters:
Placement changes tone before noise and room reflections are recorded. Correcting a poor distance with heavy EQ cannot remove captured plosives or room sound cleanly.
