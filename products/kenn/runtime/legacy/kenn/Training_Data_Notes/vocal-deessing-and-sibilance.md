# Vocal De-Essing And Sibilance

Type: Mixing workflow
Tags: vocals, de-essing, deesser, sibilance, harsh, s sounds, t sounds, sharp, compression, eq, mixing
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Treat harsh S and T sounds with a de-esser or dynamic EQ before pushing vocal brightness. Aim to control only the sharp moments, not dull the whole vocal.

Try this:
1. Find the harsh range by sweeping an EQ while the vocal says S, T, CH, or SH sounds.
2. Start checking around 5–9 kHz for most vocals, but use your ears rather than a fixed number.
3. Place a de-esser after corrective EQ and before heavy brightness boosts.
4. Set the threshold so gain reduction happens mainly on sibilant words.
5. If the whole vocal turns lisped or dark, reduce the amount or narrow the target band.
6. Re-check after compression, saturation, and reverb because those can exaggerate sibilance.

Why it matters:
Bright vocals can sound professional, but uncontrolled sibilance becomes painful on headphones and small speakers. De-essing keeps clarity without harshness.
