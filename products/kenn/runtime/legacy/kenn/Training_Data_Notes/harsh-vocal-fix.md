# Harsh Vocal Fix

Type: Mixing workflow
Tags: vocals, harsh, sibilance, de-essing, dynamic eq, eq, compression, saturation, brightness, mic technique, recording
Status: Approved

Short answer:
Harsh vocals usually need targeted control, not a blanket dark EQ — and the source of the harshness matters more than the fix. It can come from the recording (a bright/cheap mic, a performer too close and off-axis, a harsh-sounding room, a loud take driving the mic's electronics) or from the mix (sibilance, upper-mid bite, distortion, or over-compression exaggerating existing harmonics). Find which one it is before reaching for a de-esser or a cut.

Try this:
1. Bypass every vocal plugin and listen to the dry recording on its own — if it's already harsh with nothing on it, the cause is upstream of the mix (mic choice, mic distance/angle, performance, or room) and no amount of EQ will fully fix it; consider whether a re-take, different mic, or re-amp is realistically faster than chasing it in the mix.
2. If the dry vocal is fine and harshness only appears with processing, isolate which stage introduces it: bypass compression, then saturation/exciters, one at a time, and compare against the dry take.
3. If S and T sounds jump out specifically, use de-essing around the sibilant range rather than a broad cut.
4. If words bark or stab across the whole upper-mid range, try dynamic EQ in the upper mids rather than a static wide cut.
5. Check compression attack/release — fast attack with a short release can push consonants forward and read as harsh even when the tonal balance is fine.
6. Level-match after each move so a quieter vocal is not mistaken for a better vocal.

Why it matters:
Harshness that survives with everything bypassed is a recording-stage problem, not a mixing one — EQ can tame the worst of it but can't fully replace a cleaner source. Harshness that only appears under processing points at a specific plugin's behaviour, and dynamic control there keeps vocal clarity while preventing painful peaks on headphones and small speakers.
