# Boxy Vocal Correction

Type: Vocal mixing troubleshooting workflow
Tags: vocals, boxy, eq, low mids, compression, room tone, clarity, feedback repair
Status: Approved

Short answer:
A boxy vocal usually has too much low-mid energy or room resonance around 200-500 Hz. Fix the vocal tone with focused EQ and level checks before reaching for unrelated drum or master processing.

Try this:
1. High-pass only the unusable rumble first, then listen in the full mix before cutting body.
2. Use EQ Eight with a narrow bell boost to find the worst boxy resonance, often around 250-450 Hz, then cut 2-4 dB.
3. If the vocal gets thin, reduce the cut or move it to a dynamic EQ style move instead of carving the whole range.
4. Check whether compression is bringing up room tone; slower release or less gain reduction can reduce boxiness.
5. Compare the vocal against the instrumental at low volume; the words should feel clear without sounding hollow.
6. Add brightness only after the low-mid buildup is controlled, otherwise the vocal can become harsh and still boxy.

Avoid this:
- Do not answer a boxy-vocal question with drum processing.
- Do not cut every vocal around 300 Hz by default; find the actual resonance.
